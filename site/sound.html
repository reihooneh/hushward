How sound travels in a room

A sound reaches a sleeping patient three ways: straight across the room, bounced off a wall, the floor or the ceiling, and as the echo that lingers after many bounces. Move the sound and the bed, and change the room, to see which matters where.

    Close to the source the direct sound dominates and the ceiling hardly matters. Further away, the echo takes over, and that's what absorption reduces.

    Hushward is open source (MIT). It runs entirely in your browser: no sign-in, no tracking, and ward plans you load never leave your device. It uses invented example wards and patients, and its sleep model is an estimate for comparing options: measure a real ward before changing it.
